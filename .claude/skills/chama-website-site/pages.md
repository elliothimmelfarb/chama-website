# Pages and the worlds engine

- A section that is not a `.world` (the `/clients` ledger) scrolls under the fixed header, which has no background of its own. Pinned worlds never showed this because their words sit low on the screen. `/clients` gives the header a strip of paper with `html:has(main .ledger) .top::before`; any new scrolling section needs the same.
- The router (`worlds.js`) scrolls to a link's `#hash` itself, so CSS `scroll-margin-top` is ignored unless the router reads it (it does since 2026-10-22), and `pushState` never moves `:target`. An entry reached through the router gets `.is-target` instead; style both.
- A new animated page joins the router through `PAGES` in `worlds.js` and needs a `/simple` twin, both navs, the sitemap and `llms-full.txt`. `test/pages.test.js` holds the pairs and the nav labels.
- Client logos in the heroes are ink, not colour: `grayscale(1) brightness(0.62) contrast(1.5)` with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Plain `grayscale` makes gold marks (Alves) nearly vanish on paper.
- A logo a company draws in code (inline SVG plus a web font) is best captured from its own site with Playwright at `deviceScaleFactor: 4` and `omitBackground`, after clearing the backgrounds up its ancestors. Check the result for a leftover background box: Alves's header kept one, removed by keying out its colour.
- This repo is public, so a client's name or logo is published the moment it is committed and pushed, before any deploy. Keep a listing local until that company has said yes in writing.
- A drawn figure (`kind: "draw"`) is painted on the fixed canvas at its layout every frame, and only fades when a next world arrives. On a page whose last world is followed by ordinary content (`/clients`), it would stay on screen over that content. `drawKin` moves itself up with its section's pin (`pin.getBoundingClientRect().top`); a new figure in the same position needs the same.
- The `/clients` wheel (`drawWheel` in `worlds.js`) is an upright ring seen from its right: it sets each `.spoke`'s transform, depth dimming and `z-index` every frame and toggles the `.turn` in focus. There is no separate script, because the router swaps `<main>` and scripts inside it never run. It stands on the light grid world on purpose: without plates, dark logos (Toraya Lisboa, Springlar) vanish on a dark ground.
- Two text blocks crossfading in the same grid cell read as a double image mid-turn. The outgoing `.turn` leaves in 0.18s and the incoming one waits 0.2s.
- A link to a client on the wheel (`/clients#springlar`) must scroll to where that client is in focus, not to the element, which sits in a sticky pin. `wheelY()` does this for the router, same-page links, spoke clicks and a fresh load; on a fresh load it runs again after `load`, because the first measure comes too early.
- Loading `/clients#springlar` makes the browser scroll the clipped `.pin` itself (by about 90px) to reach the anchor, since `overflow: hidden` boxes still scroll programmatically. Everything inside the pin moves while the canvas does not, so the ember outline sat below the logo. `toHashTurn` resets `pin.scrollTop`; any new anchor inside a pin needs the same.
- The wheel's last place holds for `WHEEL_HOLD` of the scroll before the next world arrives. Without it the last client, or the open "Yours?" place, came into focus just as the pin started scrolling away.
- `/work` became `/clients` on 2026-10-26 and Friends moved to `/about`. The friends directory is a world with an empty pin (`.directory`), so it can stand on any dark world; on About it is the forge, because Elliot's section just before it is the night, and two sections on the same world in a row would not get the fire's handoff between their figures. The header strip behind it is keyed to that world's theme (`.top[data-theme="forge"]`).
- Since 2026-10-27 the friends directory is one screen tall on desktop (`min-height: 100svh`): the heading, deck and note on the left, the letters on the right (`.dir-list`, spanning both rows). On a phone it stacks and the note moves under the letters. A logo plate inside a flex row needs `rem` padding, because percentage padding resolves against the row's width, not the plate's, which shrank the TLC mark to a speck.
- On a phone the hero's figure only gets the room left between the header and the top of the words (`stackOf` in `worlds.js`), so every line added under the deck takes it from the shape. When the logo rows gave each label a 44px row, a 664px-tall phone left the shape 33px and it was drawn over the heading (2026-11-01). The phone block keeps the logo rows tight, and the figure may shrink to 0.22 of its size on a short phone instead of overlapping. Check 390×664 and 375×548 after adding anything to the hero.
- Elliot's phone renders the site at about 364×610 CSS px with text at roughly 106% (Chrome's font size setting). Emulating it in Playwright takes `Page.setFontSizes` through CDP (standard 17). Check every page at that size with that font before calling mobile work done. A plain 390-wide emulator hides most of the crowding.
- On a phone the header is a grid of two rows: the lockup, Members and the motion switch, then the pages. Past the opening world (`.head-compact`, set in the frame loop) the first row fades and the pages rise by `--head-lift`. `stackOf` gives every later world that height, and the `/clients` wheel world gets it almost at once. A strip behind the header (`.top::before`) has to subtract `--head-lift` too.
- Stacked world words leave fast on a phone (`.copy .r` and `.cap .r` with `--out: 1.1`), because the figure sits above them and slower words would slide up through it. The last world keeps counting past its end (`setU` with the tail) so its words fade before they reach the header.
- A drawn figure in a box (`scene.box`) never goes below 0.4 of its size: under that, its labels collide. `drawCurve` moves CHASING above the bursts when the box is short. The about prints are capped by `--fig-h`, the figure's height, so they never reach the words.
- `/privacy`'s "Where it goes" has a second, vertical SVG for phones (`.fig-flow-tall`), because the wide one's words shrink to about 5px there. Change both when the flow changes.
- Describing something we built (2026-11-11, Elliot on the first /built drafts: "Claude speak", "not really talking about what it's for or why it's there or what it can do"). The drafts he rejected described the screenshot, in two clipped sentences ending on a punchline: "A team designs its kit in 3D, adds its players and orders. The factory gets a packet ready to print." What he approved ("much better") says who it is for, what was in the way, and then what the thing can do, in ordinary connected sentences of uneven length: "Getting an order to the supplier used to take a lot of manual back and forth. We built an app where a team designs its own kit and places the order, and a command center behind it where the operators and the supplier keep track of everything." Name the whole system, not its visible page: Toraya is a website plus the staff's change-request app, and Vivir Sin Fronteras is a lead triage system, not a landing page. Ask him to say each project aloud first; his spoken description is the source.
- `/built` (2026-11-11) is one pinned world, like the `/clients` wheel: `drawDevices` in `worlds.js` toggles classes and scrolls the pictures, because scripts inside `<main>` never run after the router swaps it. Lessons from building it:
  - Cross-fading two identical device frames thins the bezels halfway. The outgoing rig stays fully opaque underneath (`.rig-was`) until the new one has faded in, and must then be removed: left in place it showed behind the next thing wherever the two rigs differ in size.
  - On a phone the devices are one size for every thing (`--fit` on the stage, set by `devicesFit`): as large as leaves the longest words clear of the progress bar and the opening words clear of the first rig. A size per thing made the devices jump between things, which the design review caught. Measure with `offsetTop`, not `getBoundingClientRect`: the hidden words carry a slide. Before the first scroll the header still has its lockup row, so the rig stands lower by `--head-lift` until `.head-compact`.
  - The pictures after the first are `data-src`, set to `src` when their thing is next, with a `<noscript>` copy. All sixteen up front is 2.2 MB. The router parses pages with scripting off, so those `<noscript>` images are real elements after a page swap: select `.screen > img`.
  - Desktop pictures are 1800 wide. At 1100 the laptop screen was soft on a 2x display.
  - On desktop every thing's words start on the line of the laptop's top (`--made-top`). Centring them made the title jump with the length of the text.
  - The things not showing are `inert`, so the progress bar's bars are links to each thing: without them nothing on the page was reachable by keyboard.
  - `.pin::before` (the scrim) is positioned, so it paints over unpositioned content in the pin: without JavaScript it washed the list out until hidden for this world.
  - Five links fit the phone header only with the tighter `.nav-link` padding in the phone block. Check `/coaching` too: it also carries Members.
  - A capture of a signed-in app comes from that project's seeded local server (VSF: `npm run build && PORT=3417 npx tsx scripts/dev-seed.ts`, then its `scripts/shots.ts`), so no real lead is ever in a picture. Show the thing we built, not the client's own website: the first VSF pictures were of the Alves landing page, which we did not make.
  - ffmpeg here has no WebP encoder. Chrome's canvas `toDataURL("image/webp")` through Playwright does the conversion.
- The router's page change (2026-11-11) is timed so a phone can read the new heading about 0.7s after the tap and everything is still by about 1.2s: `LIFT` (0.2s, measured from when the words began to leave, so a second tap never restarts it), `BURN` (0.85s, ease-out) and the `rise` keyframes, all in step with the comment above `.nav-leave`. Lessons from tightening it:
  - The shader's fire line starts 0.4 of a screen below the bottom and its flames reach about 0.3 above the line, so a burn from mix 0 spends its first quarter off screen. `MIX_FROM` starts it where the flames first touch the screen.
  - The real floor on "readable" was the hero's fresh-load fade (`.hero .copy`, 1.4s), which ran again on every swap because the hero is a new element. `html.routed` turns it off once the router has swapped a page in.
  - Anything in `.nav-enter` must end inside `BURN`: the class goes when the burn ends, and an animation still running then jumps to its end.
  - `@keyframes arrive` is defined twice in `worlds.css`; the later, opacity-only one wins everywhere, so the hero's fresh-load blur never shows.
- The router leaves `#` links to the browser, and the browser does not scroll to an anchor inside a sticky pin, because it is already "in view". The page-level click listener sends a same-page `#` link whose target has a `pinnedY` there itself (the /built progress bar), and `hashchange` runs `toHashTurn` for Back and Forward.
- Pressed states use the `scale` property, not `transform`: it composes with the transforms `worlds.js` writes every frame (the spokes) and those a control already has. `--press` in `worlds.css` is the release.
- The animated pages' speculation rules leave out the router's five pages: a speculative prefetch goes to its own cache, not the HTTP cache the router's `fetch()` reads, so both would fetch the page.
