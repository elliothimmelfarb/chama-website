# Pages and the worlds engine

The engine is `assets/worlds.js`, `assets/worlds.css` and `assets/sky.js`; the header of each says what it does. Since the 2026-11-16 rebuild there is no figure canvas: figures are inline SVG driven by `--p`, and colours come only from `assets/tokens.css`. `test/engine.test.js` holds the contracts below that a reading of the source can check, and `test/fixtures/engine.html` exercises them (serve the repo root, open `/test/fixtures/engine.html?theme=dark&motion=low`).

## The engine's API

- A `<section class="world" data-world="paper|grid|tides|ember">` is pinned unless it says `data-pin="off"`; its pin is a direct child `.pin`. An unpinned world scrolls as plain flow under the same sky, its `.r` and `[data-p]` complete, and the soft edge into the next ground stands on that section's top edge. Two sections in a row on the same ground are one world to the eye: nothing blends between them. Where there is no sky (low motion, no WebGL, one column) a change of ground gets a hairline instead, 64px above the next section. A pinned world without `--h` falls back to 150 (one and a half screens), but every built page should set it: the home page once had three pinned worlds with no hold, whose words never became readable (the test checks). In one column (and for `data-pin="off"`) the pin gets the column's padding unless its world says `data-pad="off"` (a world that pads its own contents, like /build's devices).
- `--u` and `--e` go on `U_READERS`, `--p` on `[data-p]` (a pinned world's hold, 0 to 1). `--p` is registered with initial value 1, so a figure written as "complete at 1" is whole wherever nothing writes it: no JavaScript, one column, low motion, unpinned, print. worlds.css sets it to 0 under `html.pin` before the first paint, so pinned figures and stages never flash complete.
- `[data-stage][data-duration="<s>"]` plays its `[data-p]` children from time: it starts at 60% in view but not before `data-delay` seconds after it was bound, pauses out of view and when the tab hides, never loops, and shows `data-state="idle|playing|paused|ended"`. Buttons inside: `data-stage-action="play|pause|replay"`, `data-stage-seek="<0..1>"` (the one in play gets `aria-current="step"`; `data-stage-still` on it is where low motion shows that beat), `data-stage-step="-1|1"` (previous or next seek point, `aria-disabled="true"` at the ends so focus stays), and `[data-stage-count]` reads "N of M". Arrow keys step while focus is in the stage. In low motion it is a stepper: no autoplay, it opens on its first seek point (complete if it has none), and only the seek and step controls show.
- The header's Display control (chrome.js) stores `chama-motion` (`low`/`full`) and reloads; the engine only reads `html[data-motion]`. After a router trip the engine marks the page in `.top .nav-link` and `.menu-pages a` and copies the new page's `body[data-page]`. The theme is `<html data-theme>` or the system; whatever changes it dispatches `chama:theme` on `document`, and the sky eases `uTheme` over 250 ms.
- Handlers for anything inside `<main>` are delegated from `document`: the router swaps `<main>` and scripts inside it never run. Once a swapped-in `<main>` is bound and measured, the router dispatches `chama:swapped` on `document` for other scripts' per-page setup.

## Lessons

- **The fire is gone** (2026-11-16). Elliot, after two Fable reviews: "we could get rid of the flaming transitions altogether. It's almost like it doesn't fit anymore." He agreed it belonged to the retired flame persona. The burn between sections became a soft blend of grounds (sky.js: an uneven edge a fifth of a screen wide, no rim or scorch), the router's 0.85s burn a 0.3s dissolve, low motion's torn char line a hairline. Don't bring back fire, glow rims or scorch marks as a transition.
- **One motion system** (2026-11-16, Elliot: the transitions "feel haphazard"; make them consistent to the original site's polish). Every move is one of six (DESIGN.md section 5), on the tokens in tokens.css (`--rise`, `--lift`, the `--dur-*`, `--stagger-ms`) and worlds.css `:root` (`--arrive-at`, `--arrive-k`, `--stagger`, `--fig-at`). Words arrive eased out over a quarter screen from `--arrive-at` (-0.6), each a `--stagger` after the one before; `--i`, a word's place, is written by `bind()` (words in order, then `.fig` boxes, at most 5) and never by a page. A figure box carries `fig` and arrives after the words, no earlier than `--fig-at` (-0.4; at -0.2 a phone showed a heading alone for a third of a screen and the rig failed). A page sets only `--in` (0 to 0.5) and `--out` for a beat in the hold, never `--k`, never `!important`; the test checks. Before this, `--in: -0.85` showed words already whole at the bottom edge, and three per-page overrides each invented their own timing.
- **A world leaves as one** (2026-11-16). `goneOf` gives every `.r` the same start (`leaveFrom`: `EXIT_AT` after the release, or later when the next world scrolls in as flow, so a short closing section is never alone on bare ground), then `EXIT_STAGGER` per place and an `EXIT_SPAN` fade. A part leaves earlier only where its own guard says (`mustGo`): it stands still, so the next world's first words must not reach it, and when its pin's clipping edge reaches it, it rides up with the pin only if nothing of its world is above it (desktop columns); otherwise it is gone by then (phones, where parts are stacked: a riding mark once crossed the words above it on /about). Where that makes a world go early, `measure()` writes an earlier `--arrive-at` on the next pin, so its words come as the last part fades, never before -0.85 (/build's devices on a phone left a third of a screen bare without it). `test/shots/run.mjs` fails any run of more than a quarter screen where under 12% of the screen shows something at 35% opacity or more. Check a change with an overlap probe at 364x610 (font 17): no leaving part may still show where the next world's words are.
- **Arrival is timed from where the words are** (2026-11-17). With centred frames, a world's first words sit about 40% down its pin, so a fixed `--arrive-at` faded them in below the fold while the world before had already gone: home showed up to 0.6 of a phone screen with nothing readable. `arriveOf(k)` starts a world's words as their top comes `SEEN` (90%) down the screen, written on the pin as `--arrive-at` only when later than the default, and `leaveFrom` starts the world before leaving as those words are fully in, so the two cross-fade. Counting only `.r:not(.fig)` as first words looked right and made it worse (home went from 3 fill misses to 6): keep the figure box in.
- **Figures fill their frame and share one protagonist** (2026-11-16). Elliot: "a lot of awkward empty space", and the diagrams should be "seriously elevated" to the original site's polish. Measured: most pinned worlds held 26 to 36% of a 1440x900 screen, and the pentagons were 10 to 25% of the old site's size, and flat. Now a world's contents go in a `.frame` with one of five layouts, the figure fills its cell (its size comes from the cell through `--ar`, never a fixed px), the business is `subject()` from `site/figures/kit.js` on five layers, and the shots rig's fill check reports any frame under the floor (`data-fill` on a world makes it fail). worlds.css sets no type, so the figure labels' and the statement layout's type live in base.css. A protagonist continuing from the world before passes `drawn: true` and costs nothing a frame. The ember fit sits 0.14 R outside, not the first spec's 0.2 R, which read as a second pentagon at full size. See the language at `/test/fixtures/figure.html`.
- **Home's figures are one picture carried down the page** (2026-11-16, Elliot: home should carry what we believe, our goals, values and approach, with no line said twice). Belief, can-do and approach (`site/figures/home-figures.js`) share one viewBox and one protagonist (R 130 at the origin), so the business in can-do stands where the belief left it and arrives drawn and warm. On a phone each crops its box to where it draws (`PHONE_CROP`, the svg inside an `overflow: clip` box): the uncropped 600-unit box made the business 68px, under the 72px floor. The stage's loop collapses part by part (`--eL`, `--lo` on each part), because a moving group restyles all of its children; the test now allows no exemption. 2026-11-17 review: the stage on a phone was a 140px picture the visitor scrolled past, with blank door icons until it ended; it is now 52svh (54 pushed the third door 7px under the fold, which the doors check fails) so the picture nearly takes the column, the door icons are shown from the start, and the station labels stand outside the ring (wrapping beside it). A step drawn as a filled slab read as a rendering fault (a black box in dark): draw supporting shapes in hairlines with a lit face.
- **Taste, from a board of samples** (2026-11-17). Elliot said home's diagrams and opening animation felt "amateur" and "low effort", and that the stage's text "disappears too quickly to read". On a board of five openings and five diagram styles he marked: openings, one continuous ember line Love, real product screens and an isometric workshop Maybe, Swiss poster shapes No; diagrams, technical drawing Love, hand-inked and real interface fragments Maybe, soft solid objects and bold graphic No. In words: "the hand-drawn and the technical drawing are my two favorites", and it should be "beautifully integrated" and look great on phone and desktop. So home now has the one-line stage (DESIGN.md section 11) and technical drawing figures (section 14). Line, ink and construction over shading and mass; text that stays up for its reading time. Two design judges scored each round; first round 5.5 and 6 out of 10, mostly for a line that blanked between scenes, no pen at its tip, scenes standing still while their sentence held, loud hatching and phone clipping.
- **The stage opens on what the visitor gets** (2026-11-19). Elliot found "Software companies have engineers, and their customers wait in line" dense and unclear: it opened on someone else's business and left its point for the reader to work out. From five options he picked "Big companies have their own engineers." then "Now your business can too." His own lean was "get your own engineering team" and "become the tech company"; the second clashes with "without becoming one" on the engineering door, so check that line before using it. One idea per scene: each holds about four seconds. A beat that follows "your business" must say whose it is ("We're a small...").
- **The headline is marked up by the pen** (2026-11-17). From a board of seven phone first screens Elliot picked "the line marks up the headline", and asked that "We're ready to solve any problem that can be solved with software" be the main line. The marks are inline svgs on nowrap spans in the H1, drawn by CSS after the halves arrive. Two traps: base.css caps every svg at `max-width: 100%`, which shrank the ring to its word's width until `.pen svg` set `max-width: none`; and `pathLength` dashes under `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` are unreliable in Safari, so each mark's viewBox is in hundredths of an em and its stroke is in path units. The stage waits for the pen (`data-delay` 2.6), so two ember lines never draw at once. Under 760px the first screen is one flex column ordered H1, stage, field, descriptor, sub (`.first-words` is `display: contents` there); on a 364x610 phone the field falls under the fold, and the header's Talk to us button covers it. From 760px the column-wide stage is 540px tall or more, so tablets keep the DOM order. The marks are `.pen`, never `.mark`: the header logo is `svg.mark`, and a home rule on `.mark` dashed it (the test checks).
- **The header row is full** (2026-11-18, Elliot: coaching clients must see the way into the Hearth without scrolling, so Members joined the header). Anything added to it has to be swept, not eyeballed: load every width from 300 to 1200px at default fonts of 16, 17 and 20px and check that no `.top-end` child passes the right padding and no `.nav-link` wraps, against `main` on another port for the baseline. Buttons have px heights and padding while their text is in rem, so an em breakpoint guessed from the font size is wrong; measure it. The breakpoints for the name ("Chama Inteligente", "Chama", the mark) and for the icon-only Display button are in ems for this reason. Main already overflowed at 340 to 360px with a larger font; the only known remaining overflow is 300px wide at a 20px font.
- **A world stacked in one column leaves as one** (2026-11-17, Elliot on his phone: the change from "How we work" to "Real people you can reach" "has issues especially when scrolling back up"). Each part used to stand on its own guard, so in one column the figure, which the next heading rises through, went half a screen before the words above it: a heading alone over bare ground, replayed in reverse on the way up. Now, where every part shares a column (`stacked`), all stand the distance the least able one can (`mustGo` without riding), then the world rides up with its pin as plain scroll, each part gone before the header and its shade with its first part, and a pinned next world's words come in like plain scroll too, whole by the time they show (the rule that holds them until the last part fades guards a standing world and does not apply). A first shared stand let a figure reaching past its pin ride up visibly cut, and held the next words back: /engineering-department's "The loop" on a phone then showed only its ledger for 0.37 of a screen. A first try rode a world whole only under a gap threshold; a review called it a cliff (just past it the bug remained) and it left desktop words gone under the header at once when it reached split layouts. The shared stand needs no threshold and changed nothing at 1280x720 or 1440x900. `test/shots` fails a stacked world whose parts' `--stand` differ ("stacked world split").
- **Figures finish by 75% of their hold** (2026-11-16, Fable motion review). Every scroll figure starts by about 8%, is whole between 60% and 75%, and adds nothing after 80%: on a phone the words start to leave at about 85%, and a figure that finishes later is never seen whole (the loop's closing line peaked at 60% opacity). The loop's laps are 0.19 each from 0.08 and park at 0.84.
- **Text on the grid stands on `.backed`** (2026-11-16): a radial patch of the ground's colour behind it, so the floor lines never cross words in light theme. Its inset is at most 16px sideways: wider, it made a 364px phone's page 420px wide.

- **`html.pin` collides with `.pin`** (2026-11-16). The plan renamed `html.js` to `html.pin`, and the bare `.pin { position: sticky; height: 100svh; overflow: hidden }` rule then styled `<html>`: the page became one clipped screen whose `scrollY` still moved, so the sky changed while nothing on screen scrolled. Every rule for the pin element is `.world > .pin`, and the engine finds a pin with `closest(".world > .pin")` (`closest(".pin")` returns `<html>` outside a pin and `scrollTop = 0` on it jumps to the top). The test fails on a selector whose first compound is a bare `.pin`.
- **The pinned layout** is `html.pin`, set before the first paint by `site/parts/boot.js` (`PIN_QUERY`, and not `data-motion="low"`) and again by `pinnable()`; both must use the same query (the test checks). Waiting for the deferred script painted the one-column layout first. The engine marks `html[data-engine="on"]` as it starts; if that is missing 4s after the DOM is ready, the boot snippet takes `pin` back, because pinned words stay hidden until the engine writes `--u`. Without it (no JavaScript, height 500px and under, print, low motion) the worlds are one column. `layFlat()` undoes what the loop applied when the layout leaves pinned (inline `--p`, /build's inert things and picture scrolls); anything new the loop writes on a pinned world needs its undo there. Check one column at 610x364 and 640x360, and resize from pinned to one column and back.
- **No `offset-path` along a long path in a stage.** A nib riding each scene's whole path by `offset-path` made the opening's frames crawl. A dot that rides a path is the round cap of a dash too short to see (`stroke-dasharray: 0.0001 2`, moved by `stroke-dashoffset`), as the old stage's marker was.
- **Anything written every frame lands only on the elements that read it, and never on a container.** Chrome restyles everything inside an element whose custom property changes, inherited or not (measured 2026-11-12: the pin as a reader cost every element of both worlds in view each frame). So the shade behind the words is an element (`.pin-shade`), `--p` goes on each `[data-p]` path rather than the svg, and /build's stage fades by an opacity written with `put()` only when it changes. A figure's svg may itself be `.r` to fade with its world: its `[data-p]` parts restyle each frame of the hold anyway, so keep its other children few.
- **A settled reader is written once** (2026-11-17). Making figure boxes `.fig.r` put a whole figure's subtree on `--u`, which changes every frame of a hold: home went from 68.5 to 80.5 elements a frame at 1440x900 even though the boxes' style never changed mid-hold. `measure()` now works out each reader's `settle` stretch (fully in, not yet at its `--out`; the exit is `--e`, written apart) and `setU` holds `--u` at its start there, writing only when the held value changes: 47.4 at 1440x900 and 57 at 364x610. A new reader whose style reads `--u` some other way (not `.r`, not `.pin-shade`) gets no stretch and is written every frame, as before.
- **Nothing holds a position against the scroll from a frame** (2026-11-17, Elliot on his phone: "a lot of things bouncing up and down or weird kind of magnet points" on home). A phone scrolls on its compositor, a frame or more ahead of any script, so a word held still by a translate written in rAF rode up with the page and snapped back: a touch fling at 4x CPU showed a held heading jump 20 to 39px between frames, while the same positions read perfectly in a scrollTo-and-measure probe (which never shows lag). A leaving word's stand is now an animation on `scroll(root)` (`--rel`, `--stand`, `data-stand`, written by `measure()`), with the old `--sx`/`--pl` frame path only where `animation-timeline` is unsupported. Anything new that must track the scroll goes on a scroll timeline or on native sticky. `shiftOf` subtracts both `transform` and `translate`: a measure taken past a stretch otherwise reads every stand from where it stands (it gave 3px for 284px and put words under the header on /engineering-department; the shots rig's stand check now catches it). Check it with the screencast rig: colour one element magenta, `Input.synthesizeScrollGesture` with `gestureSourceType: "touch"`, decode each `Page.screencastFrame` and track the element's top.
- **Measure frame cost in elements, not milliseconds.** Rig: a 4000px touch scroll (`Input.synthesizeScrollGesture`, 1500 px/s) at 4x CPU, sum `elementCount` of `UpdateLayoutTree` trace events, divide by `FireAnimationFrame` events; it holds within a few percent where milliseconds swing 30%. Throw away a run that did not scroll the whole way or caught under 50 frames: the trace and the synthetic gesture sometimes miss each other (a `scrollTo(0,0)` just before tracing made every run miss). In zsh, `set -- $u` does not split a string, so a loop over "url w h" passes it all as the URL. 2026-11-16 after the motion system: `/` at 364x610 50.1 elements a frame against 50.2 for the engine before it on the same machine and Chrome (67.6 against 68.3 at 1440x900); this machine reads about 8 more than the run below, so compare before and after on one machine. Earlier, HEAD's `/` markup: old engine 60.8 elements and 2.96 ms of style a frame at 364x610 (71.6 and 4.08 at 1440x900); new engine 41.9 and 1.94 (43.4 and 2.28). The fixture: about 17.7 and 1.2 at both sizes.
- **The loop reads no layout.** Positions come from `measure()` (reads, then writes through `setVar`, which skips unchanged values, then the second round of reads), the scroll from its event, and a ResizeObserver re-measures a page that grows. A layout read in any script's animation frame forces that frame's style, so new code reads in the scroll event or `measure()`.
- **A frame-loop local named like a closure variable breaks the engine silently** (`var last` once shadowed the frame timer and the sky went black). Look at a screenshot after any change to the loop.
- **Leaving a pin.** What rides up with a leaving pin is gone before it reaches the header and dissolves rather than blinks: the fade (`--e`, written by `setU` only when it changes) always lasts `EXIT_SPAN`, shifted earlier into the hold where the room is short. The last world counts on past its end only as far as the footer (`reach`). Judge an exit from frames around each release at 364x610 (font 17) and 1440x900, not from a static screenshot of the page's end.
- **The idle throttle.** A still page draws every other frame at 120 Hz (`STILL_MS`, `AMBIENT_MS`). Anything that must move every frame on a still page marks itself in `stirred()` (the theme ease does). Stages run their own rAF only while playing.
- **The sky** has four grounds, each a light and a dark palette at the top of `sky.js`, mixed by `uTheme`. Each ground's main colour is its `--ground` in tokens.css (the test checks within 0.025), so low motion, no WebGL and the sky agree. A palette entry is a pair of colours or strengths; `T(name, key)` writes the mix into the shader. Anything past the last ground is ember (`sparks.js`, the old 404's game, passed ids up to 5; no page loads it since the rebuild).
- **Elliot's phone** renders at about 364x610 CSS px with text at about 106% (`Page.setFontSizes`, standard 17, through CDP). Check every page there; a plain 390-wide emulator hides most of the crowding.

## The router

- It scrolls to a link's `#hash` itself, reading `scroll-margin-top`, and `pushState` never moves `:target`: an entry reached through it gets `.is-target`; style both. An anchor inside a pin is reached through `anchorY()`/`pinnedY()` (where it is in focus), which answer `null` outside the pinned layout so the scroll falls back to the element; a new "in focus" helper must do the same. The browser does not scroll to an anchor already "in view" in a sticky pin, so same-page `#` links with a `pinnedY` are handled by the page-level click listener, and `hashchange` by `toHashTurn`. Loading with a hash makes the browser scroll the clipped pin itself; `toHashTurn` resets its `scrollTop`.
- `PAGES` in worlds.js is `site/site.js`'s `inRouter` list (the test checks). A page swaps in only if its scripts and stylesheets are already loaded (`sameDeploy`); anything else is a full load. Low motion has no router.
- Timing: the words lift for `LIFT` (0.2s, from when they began to leave), the sky dissolves into the new ground over `FADE` (0.3s, `uFade` in sky.js), and `.nav-enter` stays for `ENTER` (0.7s) so its rise animations finish before the class goes: removing a class under a running animation restarts or cuts it. The H1's shorter replay hangs on `html.routed`, which never comes off, for the same reason. It measures the new page only once `.nav-leave` (a `--lift`) is gone. The rise reaches every pinned world's words by `--i` (`html.nav-enter main .world > .pin .r`), not only `.copy`; under reduced motion they fade over `--dur-quick` and the sky still dissolves. A page left by a full load goes into the back/forward cache mid-change; `pageshow` with `persisted` settles it.
- The cross-document dissolve is opted into by an inline `<style>` under `<title>`: an `@view-transition` in a stylesheet still loading counts as none. Test with CDP network throttling, never Playwright's `route()`, which aborts every cross-document transition.
- Speculation rules leave out the router's pages (a prefetch goes to its own cache, so both would fetch), and list only named public pages: a prefetch carries cookies, and `/*` reached GETs that change state, such as a magic link's callback.

## /build's devices (drawDevices)

- Cross-fading two identical device frames thins the bezels halfway: the outgoing rig stays opaque underneath (`.rig-was`) until the new one has faded in, then goes.
- On a phone the devices are one size for every thing (`--fit`, `devicesFit`, found by halving thirteen sizes), measured with `offsetTop`, not `getBoundingClientRect`, because the hidden words carry a slide.
- Pictures after the first are `data-src`/`data-srcset`, set when their thing is next, with `<noscript>` copies (real elements after the router parses a page: select `.screen > img`). Neighbours load only once the pair on screen has arrived. Desktop pictures are 1800 wide, phones get copies through `srcset`; check `srcset` picks in the installed Chrome, since Playwright's Chromium picks differently.
- The last thing's pictures run on `devicesLast` until the pin lets go, ending at the page's own speed, so the scroll never has a dead stretch (Elliot, 2026-11-12). Measure it as numbers per scroll step, not by eye.
- The things not showing are `inert`; the progress bar's bars are links to each thing.
- The devices world opens the page (2026-11-16, Elliot: "a lot of awkward empty space"; the H1 alone sat over a laptop peeking from the bottom edge). The H1, line and field are the `.made-open`, beside the first thing's devices, and the opening stretch is the section's `data-open` (40, against 135 a thing): a long still opening at the very top of a page reads as a scroll that does nothing. `--h` is 100 + `data-open` + 135 a thing (the test checks).
- When the devices may leave is set by the foot of the last thing (its rig and words): they must be gone before the next world's words reach it, and those words only come into view from near the screen's bottom. A taller rig or longer last words leave bare ground between the worlds (on a 364x610 phone, 60px more foot made a half screen under the fill floor). So the last thing's words are kept short on a phone, and on a short desktop the rig is capped by height.

## Content and logos

- This repo is public: a client's name or logo is published the moment it is committed and pushed.
- Logos are ink, not colour: `grayscale(1) brightness(0.62) contrast(1.5)` with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; plain grayscale makes gold marks vanish. Each needs its own dark-theme treatment. A logo a company draws in code is best captured from its own site with Playwright at `deviceScaleFactor: 4` and `omitBackground`.
- Chrome scores LCP when an element first paints with any opacity, so entrance animations start at opacity 0.01, not 0.
- Pressed states use the `scale` property, not `transform`, so they compose with transforms the engine writes.
- A section that scrolls under the fixed header (an unpinned world, a ledger) needs a strip behind the header; pinned words never showed the problem because they sit low.
- Describing something we built (2026-11-11, Elliot on the first /built drafts: "Claude speak", "not really talking about what it's for or why it's there or what it can do"). Say who it is for, what was in the way, then what it can do, in ordinary connected sentences of uneven length, and name the whole system, not its visible page. Approved: "Getting an order to the supplier used to take a lot of manual back and forth. We built an app where a team designs its own kit and places the order, and a command center behind it where the operators and the supplier keep track of everything." When he has described a project aloud, his description is the source.
- Captures of signed-in apps come from that project's seeded local server, so no real lead is in a picture. ffmpeg here has no WebP encoder; Chrome's canvas `toDataURL("image/webp")` through Playwright does the conversion.
