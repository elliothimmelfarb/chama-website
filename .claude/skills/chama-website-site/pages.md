# Pages and the worlds engine

This file says how the engine works and what has gone wrong in it. It does not say how the site should look. The purpose, Elliot's decisions and his reactions are in `docs/DESIGN.md`, and the look is the designer's to decide. Where a section here mentions a design choice, it says what was built and who chose it. Those choices can change, and the tests that pin them change with them.

The engine is `assets/worlds.js`, `assets/worlds.css` and `assets/sky.js`; the header of each says what it does. Since the 2026-11-16 rebuild there is no figure canvas: figures are inline SVG driven by `--p`, and colours come only from `assets/tokens.css`. `test/engine.test.js` holds the contracts below that a reading of the source can check, and `test/fixtures/engine.html` exercises them (serve the repo root, open `/test/fixtures/engine.html?theme=dark&motion=low`).

## The engine's API

- A `<section class="world" data-world="paper|grid|tides|ember">` is pinned unless it says `data-pin="off"`; its pin is a direct child `.pin`. An unpinned world scrolls as plain flow under the same sky, its `.r` and `[data-p]` complete, and the soft edge into the next ground stands on that section's top edge. Two sections in a row on the same ground are one world to the eye: nothing blends between them. Where there is no sky (low motion, no WebGL, one column) a change of ground gets a hairline instead, 64px above the next section. A pinned world without `--h` falls back to 150 (one and a half screens). Home once had three pinned worlds with no hold, whose words never became readable, so the test now fails a pinned world on a built page that sets none. In one column (and for `data-pin="off"`) the pin gets the column's padding unless its world says `data-pad="off"` (a world that pads its own contents, like /build's devices).
- `--u` and `--e` go on `U_READERS`, `--p` on `[data-p]` (a pinned world's hold, 0 to 1). `--p` is registered with initial value 1, so a figure written as "complete at 1" is whole wherever nothing writes it: no JavaScript, one column, low motion, unpinned, print. worlds.css sets it to 0 under `html.pin` before the first paint, so pinned figures and stages never flash complete.
- `[data-stage][data-duration="<s>"]` plays its `[data-p]` children from time: it starts at 60% in view but not before `data-delay` seconds after it was bound, pauses out of view and when the tab hides, never loops, and shows `data-state="idle|playing|paused|ended"`. Buttons inside: `data-stage-action="play|pause|replay"`, `data-stage-seek="<0..1>"` (the one in play gets `aria-current="step"`; `data-stage-still` on it is where low motion shows that beat), `data-stage-step="-1|1"` (previous or next seek point, `aria-disabled="true"` at the ends so focus stays), and `[data-stage-count]` reads "N of M". Arrow keys step while focus is in the stage. In low motion it is a stepper: no autoplay, it opens on its first seek point (complete if it has none), and only the seek and step controls show.
- The header's Display control (chrome.js) stores `chama-motion` (`low`/`full`) and reloads; the engine only reads `html[data-motion]`. After a router trip the engine marks the page in `.top .nav-link` and `.menu-pages a` and copies the new page's `body[data-page]`. The theme is `<html data-theme>` or the system; whatever changes it dispatches `chama:theme` on `document`, and the sky eases `uTheme` over 250 ms.
- Handlers for anything inside `<main>` are delegated from `document`, because the router swaps `<main>` and scripts inside it never run. Once a swapped-in `<main>` is bound and measured, the router dispatches `chama:swapped` on `document` for other scripts' per-page setup.
- A world's contents go in a `.frame` with a `data-layout` (five exist in worlds.css). A figure's size comes from its cell through `--ar`. worlds.css sets no type, so the figure labels' and the statement layout's type live in base.css.

## How arriving and leaving work, and why

Each of these was built to fix something Elliot or a review saw. The mechanism is described so a change to it knows what it was holding up.

- **Timing lives in one place** (2026-11-16). Elliot said the transitions "feel haphazard" and asked for the original site's consistency. The cause was three per-page overrides that each invented their own timing, and an `--in: -0.85` that showed words already whole at the bottom edge. Timing now comes from tokens.css (`--rise`, `--lift`, the `--dur-*`, `--stagger-ms`) and worlds.css `:root` (`--arrive-at`, `--arrive-k`, `--stagger`, `--fig-at`). Words arrive eased out over a quarter screen from `--arrive-at` (-0.6), each a `--stagger` after the one before. `--i`, a word's place, is written by `bind()` (words in order, then `.fig` boxes, at most 5) and by no page; the test fails a `.r` that sets its own. tokens.css names the six moves everything uses. A figure box carries `fig` and arrives after the words, no earlier than `--fig-at` (-0.4; at -0.2 a phone showed a heading alone for a third of a screen and the rig failed). A page sets `--in` (0 to 0.5) and `--out` for a beat in the hold. The test fails a page that sets `--k`, or forces `--in` or `--out` with `!important`, because that is how the per-page timings crept in.
- **A world leaves as one** (2026-11-16). `goneOf` gives every `.r` the same start (`leaveFrom`: `EXIT_AT` after the release, or later when the next world scrolls in as flow, so a short closing section is never alone on bare ground), then `EXIT_STAGGER` per place and an `EXIT_SPAN` fade. A part leaves earlier only where its own guard says (`mustGo`). It stands still, so the next world's first words would otherwise reach it. When its pin's clipping edge reaches it, it rides up with the pin only if nothing of its world is above it (desktop columns); otherwise it is gone by then (phones, where parts are stacked: a riding mark once crossed the words above it on /about). Where that makes a world go early, `measure()` writes an earlier `--arrive-at` on the next pin, so its words come as the last part fades, never before -0.85 (/build's devices on a phone left a third of a screen bare without it).
- **Arrival is timed from where the words are** (2026-11-17). With centred frames, a world's first words sit about 40% down its pin, so a fixed `--arrive-at` faded them in below the fold while the world before had already gone: home showed up to 0.6 of a phone screen with nothing readable. `arriveOf(k)` starts a world's words as their top comes `SEEN` (90%) down the screen, written on the pin as `--arrive-at` only when later than the default, and `leaveFrom` starts the world before leaving as those words are fully in, so the two cross-fade. Counting only `.r:not(.fig)` as first words looked right and made it worse (home went from 3 fill misses to 6), which is why the figure box counts.
- **A world stacked in one column leaves as one** (2026-11-17). Elliot on his phone: the change from "How we work" to "Real people you can reach" "has issues especially when scrolling back up". Each part used to stand on its own guard, so in one column the figure, which the next heading rises through, went half a screen before the words above it: a heading alone over bare ground, replayed in reverse on the way up. Now, where every part shares a column (`stacked`), all stand the distance the least able one can (`mustGo` without riding), then the world rides up with its pin as plain scroll, each part gone before the header and its shade with its first part, and a pinned next world's words come in like plain scroll too, whole by the time they show (what holds them until the last part fades guards a standing world and does not apply here). A first shared stand let a figure reaching past its pin ride up visibly cut, and held the next words back: /engineering-department's "The loop" on a phone then showed only its ledger for 0.37 of a screen. A first try rode a world whole only under a gap threshold; a review called it a cliff (just past it the bug remained) and it left desktop words gone under the header at once when it reached split layouts. The shared stand needs no threshold and changed nothing at 1280x720 or 1440x900. `test/shots` fails a stacked world whose parts' `--stand` differ ("stacked world split").
- **Leaving a pin.** What rides up with a leaving pin is gone before it reaches the header, and it dissolves instead of blinking: the fade (`--e`, written by `setU` only when it changes) always lasts `EXIT_SPAN`, shifted earlier into the hold where the room is short. The last world counts on past its end only as far as the footer (`reach`). A static screenshot of the page's end does not show an exit. Frames around each release at 364x610 (font 17) and 1440x900 do.
- **On a phone the words start to leave at about 85% of the hold** (2026-11-16, a motion review). A figure that finishes later than that is never seen whole there: the loop's closing line once peaked at 60% opacity. The figures as built start by about 8% and are whole between 60% and 75%, adding nothing after 80% (the loop's laps are 0.19 each from 0.08 and park at 0.84).
- **What the shots rig measures.** `test/shots/run.mjs` fails any run of more than a quarter screen where under 12% of the screen shows something at 35% opacity or more (dead scroll), and reports any frame that fills less of the screen than its floor (`data-fill` on a world makes that a failure). These floors were set after Elliot's "a lot of awkward empty space" on 2026-11-16, when most pinned worlds held 26 to 36% of a 1440x900 screen. They describe that fix, and a design that uses space differently changes them. It also fails `/` on the phone if any of the three doors is off the 610px screen after one screen of scroll, and checks that the desktop first view is one screen. An overlap probe at 364x610 (font 17) shows whether a leaving part is still visible where the next world's words are.

## Frame cost and scrolling

- **Chrome restyles everything inside an element whose custom property changes, inherited or not** (measured 2026-11-12: the pin as a reader cost every element of both worlds in view each frame). So anything written every frame lands on the elements that read it and not on a container: the shade behind the words is an element (`.pin-shade`), `--p` goes on each `[data-p]` path and not on the svg, and /build's stage fades by an opacity written with `put()` only when it changes. A figure's svg may itself be `.r` to fade with its world: its `[data-p]` parts restyle each frame of the hold anyway, so its other children are kept few. A moving group restyles all of its children for the same reason; `test/figures.test.js` lets a `<g data-p>` hold at most three descendants and lists no exceptions.
- **A settled reader is written once** (2026-11-17). Making figure boxes `.fig.r` put a whole figure's subtree on `--u`, which changes every frame of a hold: home went from 68.5 to 80.5 elements a frame at 1440x900 even though the boxes' style never changed mid-hold. `measure()` now works out each reader's `settle` stretch (fully in, not yet at its `--out`; the exit is `--e`, written apart) and `setU` holds `--u` at its start there, writing only when the held value changes: 47.4 at 1440x900 and 57 at 364x610 on home as it was then. A new reader whose style reads `--u` some other way (not `.r`, not `.pin-shade`) gets no stretch and is written every frame.
- **A phone scrolls ahead of any script** (2026-11-17, Elliot on his phone: "a lot of things bouncing up and down or weird kind of magnet points" on home). A phone scrolls on its compositor, a frame or more ahead of scripts, so a word held still by a translate written in rAF rode up with the page and snapped back: a touch fling at 4x CPU showed a held heading jump 20 to 39px between frames, while the same positions read perfectly in a scrollTo-and-measure probe (which never shows lag). A leaving word's stand is now an animation on `scroll(root)` (`--rel`, `--stand`, `data-stand`, written by `measure()`), with the old `--sx`/`--pl` frame path only where `animation-timeline` is unsupported. Scroll timelines and native sticky are the two things that track a phone's scroll without lag. `shiftOf` subtracts both `transform` and `translate`: a measure taken past a stretch otherwise reads every stand from where it stands (it gave 3px for 284px and put words under the header on /engineering-department; the shots rig's stand check now catches it). The screencast rig shows the lag: colour one element magenta, `Input.synthesizeScrollGesture` with `gestureSourceType: "touch"`, decode each `Page.screencastFrame` and track the element's top.
- **Frame cost reads steadier in elements than in milliseconds.** Rig: a 4000px touch scroll (`Input.synthesizeScrollGesture`, 1500 px/s) at 4x CPU, sum `elementCount` of `UpdateLayoutTree` trace events, divide by `FireAnimationFrame` events; it holds within a few percent where milliseconds swing 30%. A run that did not scroll the whole way or caught under 50 frames is noise: the trace and the synthetic gesture sometimes miss each other (a `scrollTo(0,0)` just before tracing made every run miss). In zsh, `set -- $u` does not split a string, so a loop over "url w h" passes it all as the URL. 2026-11-16 after the timing change: `/` at 364x610 50.1 elements a frame against 50.2 for the engine before it on the same machine and Chrome (67.6 against 68.3 at 1440x900); that machine reads about 8 more than the run below, so only a before and after on one machine compare. Earlier, on the same `/` markup: old engine 60.8 elements and 2.96 ms of style a frame at 364x610 (71.6 and 4.08 at 1440x900); new engine 41.9 and 1.94 (43.4 and 2.28). The fixture: about 17.7 and 1.2 at both sizes. Home has no pinned worlds since 2026-11-19, so none of the numbers for `/` in this section are today's.
- **The loop reads no layout.** Positions come from `measure()` (reads, then writes through `setVar`, which skips unchanged values, then the second round of reads), the scroll from its event, and a ResizeObserver re-measures a page that grows. A layout read in any script's animation frame forces that frame's style, which is why reads happen in the scroll event or `measure()`.
- **A frame-loop local named like a closure variable breaks the engine silently** (`var last` once shadowed the frame timer and the sky went black). A screenshot after a change to the loop shows it; the tests do not.
- **The idle throttle.** A still page draws every other frame at 120 Hz (`STILL_MS`, `AMBIENT_MS`). Anything that must move every frame on a still page marks itself in `stirred()` (the theme ease does). Stages run their own rAF only while playing.
- **`offset-path` along a long path in a stage is slow.** A nib riding each scene's whole path by `offset-path` made the opening's frames crawl. The dot that rides a path now is the round cap of a dash too short to see (`stroke-dasharray: 0.0001 2`, moved by `stroke-dashoffset`), as the old stage's marker was.

## Layout traps

- **`html.pin` collides with `.pin`** (2026-11-16). The plan renamed `html.js` to `html.pin`, and the bare `.pin { position: sticky; height: 100svh; overflow: hidden }` rule then styled `<html>`: the page became one clipped screen whose `scrollY` still moved, so the sky changed while nothing on screen scrolled. Every rule for the pin element is `.world > .pin`, and the engine finds a pin with `closest(".world > .pin")` (`closest(".pin")` returns `<html>` outside a pin and `scrollTop = 0` on it jumps to the top). The test fails on a selector whose first compound is a bare `.pin`.
- **The pinned layout** is `html.pin`, set before the first paint by `site/parts/boot.js` (`PIN_QUERY`, and not `data-motion="low"`) and again by `pinnable()`; the two use the same query (the test checks). Waiting for the deferred script painted the one-column layout first. The engine marks `html[data-engine="on"]` as it starts; if that is missing 4s after the DOM is ready, the boot snippet takes `pin` back, because pinned words stay hidden until the engine writes `--u`. Without it (no JavaScript, height 500px and under, print, low motion) the worlds are one column. `layFlat()` undoes what the loop applied when the layout leaves pinned (inline `--p`, /build's inert things and picture scrolls); anything new the loop writes on a pinned world has no undo until it is added there. One column shows at 610x364 and 640x360, and on a resize from pinned to one column and back.
- **Elliot's phone** renders at about 364x610 CSS px with text at about 106% (`Page.setFontSizes`, standard 17, through CDP). A plain 390-wide emulator hides most of the crowding he sees.
- **The header row is full** (2026-11-18, when Members joined it). Buttons have px heights and padding while their text is in rem, so an em breakpoint guessed from the font size is wrong, and the breakpoints for the name ("Chama Inteligente", "Chama", the mark) and for the icon-only Display button are in ems and were found by measuring. What caught the overflows was a sweep: every width from 300 to 1200px at default fonts of 16, 17 and 20px, checking that no `.top-end` child passes the right padding and no `.nav-link` wraps, against `main` on another port for the baseline. Main already overflowed at 340 to 360px with a larger font; the only known remaining overflow is 300px wide at a 20px font.
- **A page class can collide with base.css.** `.qa` is the objections list there (a bordered `details`); home's question sections took the name and drew a full-width hairline under every section. They are `home-q` now.
- **The header logo is `svg.mark`.** A home rule on `.mark` once dashed it, which is why the pen's marks are `.pen`. The test fails home styles that select the logo's classes.
- **`.backed`** puts a radial patch of the ground's colour behind text on the grid, so the floor lines do not cross words in light theme. With an inset wider than 16px sideways it made a 364px phone's page 420px wide.
- **A section that scrolls under the fixed header** (an unpinned world, a ledger) shows through it without a strip behind the header; pinned words never showed the problem because they sit low.
- **Pressed states use the `scale` property**, not `transform`, so they compose with transforms the engine writes.
- **Chrome scores LCP when an element first paints with any opacity**, so entrance animations start at opacity 0.01, not 0.

## The sky

Four grounds, each a light and a dark palette at the top of `sky.js`, mixed by `uTheme`. Each ground's main colour is its `--ground` in tokens.css (the test checks within 0.025), so low motion, no WebGL and the sky agree. A palette entry is a pair of colours or strengths; `T(name, key)` writes the mix into the shader. Anything past the last ground is ember (`sparks.js`, the old 404's game, passed ids up to 5; the file is gone, though sky.js's header still names it). The change between grounds is a soft blend (an uneven edge a fifth of a screen wide), the router's change a 0.3s dissolve, and low motion's a hairline. These replaced the fire Elliot retired on 2026-11-16 (`docs/DESIGN.md` has his words).

## Home's first view

Elliot calls this part of the rebuild "awesome" (2026-11-19). How it came about is in `docs/DESIGN.md`; this is how it is built.

- **The stage.** One continuous ember line, picked by Elliot from a board of openings. He said its text "disappears too quickly to read", and each scene now holds for its reading time (`readingTime` in `site/figures/stage.js`, about four seconds for these lines). He found the first opening line ("Software companies have engineers, and their customers wait in line") dense, and from five options picked "Big companies have their own engineers." then "Now your business can too." His own lean was "get your own engineering team" and "become the tech company"; the second clashes with "without becoming one" on the engineering door. A review found that the beat after "your business" left it unclear whose business was meant, so that beat says who we are: since 2026-11-19 "We're those engineers, and we build with AI." Elliot had it reworded from "We're a small, family-owned, AI-native business." because the line under the H1 already says small family business, and `test/stage.test.js` fails a scene that says "family" or "Lisbon". The stage waits for the pen (`data-delay` 2.6), so two ember lines never draw at once. Two design judges scored the first round 5.5 and 6 out of 10, mostly for a line that blanked between scenes, no pen at its tip, scenes standing still while their sentence held, loud hatching and phone clipping.
- **The pen on the headline.** From a board of seven phone first screens Elliot picked "the line marks up the headline", and asked that "We're ready to solve any problem that can be solved with software" be the main line. The marks are inline svgs on nowrap spans in the H1, drawn by CSS after the halves arrive. Two traps: base.css caps every svg at `max-width: 100%`, which shrank the ring to its word's width until `.pen svg` set `max-width: none`; and `pathLength` dashes under `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` are unreliable in Safari, so each mark's viewBox is in hundredths of an em and its stroke is in path units.
- **A pen mark is placed by the ink, not the box.** An absolutely placed svg inside an inline span is positioned against the font's content area (1.25em tall in Funnel Display), not the letters, so `top: 0; height: 100%` sits the ring low and cuts the descenders' line. The word's ink runs from the cap top at about 0.31em to the baseline at about 0.94em of the span. The ring is a flattened pill: an ellipse that clears a word's corners is far wider than the gap to the next word. The ringed span takes a little inline margin so the loop clears "with".
- **Order by width.** Under 760px the first screen is one flex column ordered H1, stage, field, descriptor, sub (`.first-words` is `display: contents` there); on a 364x610 phone the field falls under the fold, and the header's Talk to us button covers it. From 760px the column-wide stage is 540px tall or more, so tablets keep the DOM order. From 1100px the first view down to the "also" line is one screen. The cycling phrases hold 2s at every width.
- **Below the first view** (2026-11-19) home is four questions and the close, all on paper and unpinned, with no figure after the stage. Elliot picked the questions format, paper only, no diagrams and one flowing scroll on phones; four questions and a close, and the pen marking one phrase in each answer, were the session's calls (the second a design reviewer's idea). His picks are on the artifact "Chama Homepage Picks" (https://claude.ai/artifact/H9Lzf2LDNrjTDGenNv9xmz, its `picks` collection). He left the length open with a note: don't overwhelm people, and complement the other pages. The H1's pen marks one phrase in each answer on a `view()` scroll timeline (whole in low and reduced motion). `.first` scopes the H1's time-based pen animations, so they never reach the answers' marks.

## The router

- It scrolls to a link's `#hash` itself, reading `scroll-margin-top`, and `pushState` never moves `:target`: an entry reached through it gets `.is-target`, so a target style needs both selectors. An anchor inside a pin is reached through `anchorY()`/`pinnedY()` (where it is in focus), which answer `null` outside the pinned layout so the scroll falls back to the element (any other helper that answers "where is this in focus" needs the same null). The browser does not scroll to an anchor already "in view" in a sticky pin, so same-page `#` links with a `pinnedY` are handled by the page-level click listener, and `hashchange` by `toHashTurn`. Loading with a hash makes the browser scroll the clipped pin itself; `toHashTurn` resets its `scrollTop`.
- `PAGES` in worlds.js is `site/site.js`'s `inRouter` list (the test checks). A page swaps in only if its scripts and stylesheets are already loaded (`sameDeploy`); anything else is a full load. Low motion has no router.
- Timing: the words lift for `LIFT` (0.2s, from when they began to leave), the sky dissolves into the new ground over `FADE` (0.3s, `uFade` in sky.js), and `.nav-enter` stays for `ENTER` (0.7s) so its rise animations finish before the class goes: removing a class under a running animation restarts or cuts it. The H1's shorter replay hangs on `html.routed`, which never comes off, for the same reason. It measures the new page only once `.nav-leave` (a `--lift`) is gone. The rise reaches every pinned world's words by `--i` (`html.nav-enter main .world > .pin .r`), not only `.copy`; under reduced motion they fade over `--dur-quick` and the sky still dissolves. A page left by a full load goes into the back/forward cache mid-change; `pageshow` with `persisted` settles it.
- The cross-document dissolve is opted into by an inline `<style>` under `<title>`: an `@view-transition` in a stylesheet still loading counts as none. Playwright's `route()` aborts every cross-document transition, so CDP network throttling is what tests it.
- Speculation rules leave out the router's pages (a prefetch goes to its own cache, so both would fetch), and list only named public pages: a prefetch carries cookies, and `/*` reached GETs that change state, such as a magic link's callback.

## /build's devices (drawDevices)

- Cross-fading two identical device frames thins the bezels halfway: the outgoing rig stays opaque underneath (`.rig-was`) until the new one has faded in, then goes.
- On a phone the devices are one size for every thing (`--fit`, `devicesFit`, found by halving thirteen sizes), measured with `offsetTop`, not `getBoundingClientRect`, because the hidden words carry a slide.
- Pictures after the first are `data-src`/`data-srcset`, set when their thing is next, with `<noscript>` copies (real elements after the router parses a page: select `.screen > img`). Neighbours load only once the pair on screen has arrived. Desktop pictures are 1800 wide, phones get copies through `srcset`. Playwright's Chromium picks from `srcset` differently from the installed Chrome.
- The last thing's pictures run on `devicesLast` until the pin lets go, ending at the page's own speed, so the scroll never has a dead stretch (Elliot, 2026-11-12). It shows in numbers per scroll step, not by eye.
- The things not showing are `inert`; the progress bar's bars are links to each thing.
- The devices world opens the page (2026-11-16, Elliot: "a lot of awkward empty space"; the H1 alone sat over a laptop peeking from the bottom edge). The H1, line and field are the `.made-open`, beside the first thing's devices, and the opening stretch is the section's `data-open` (40, against 135 a thing): a long still opening at the very top of a page reads as a scroll that does nothing. `--h` is 100 + `data-open` + 135 a thing (the test checks).
- When the devices may leave is set by the foot of the last thing (its rig and words): they must be gone before the next world's words reach it, and those words only come into view from near the screen's bottom. A taller rig or longer last words leave bare ground between the worlds (on a 364x610 phone, 60px more foot made a half screen under the fill floor). That is why the last thing's words are short on a phone, and why the rig is capped by height on a short desktop.

## Content and logos

- This repo is public: a client's name or logo is published the moment it is committed and pushed.
- Logos are shown as ink: `grayscale(1) brightness(0.62) contrast(1.5)` with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; plain grayscale makes gold marks vanish. Each has its own dark-theme treatment. A logo a company draws in code captures best from its own site with Playwright at `deviceScaleFactor: 4` and `omitBackground`.
- Describing something we built (2026-11-11, Elliot on the first /built drafts: "Claude speak", "not really talking about what it's for or why it's there or what it can do"). What he approved says who it is for, what was in the way, then what it can do, in ordinary connected sentences of uneven length, and names the whole system, not its visible page: "Getting an order to the supplier used to take a lot of manual back and forth. We built an app where a team designs its own kit and places the order, and a command center behind it where the operators and the supplier keep track of everything." When he has described a project aloud, his description is the source.
- Captures of signed-in apps come from that project's seeded local server, so no real lead is in a picture. ffmpeg here has no WebP encoder; Chrome's canvas `toDataURL("image/webp")` through Playwright does the conversion.
