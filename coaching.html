The shortest path up the learning curve

Experience matters more than the tool.

AI platforms add new models, settings, and features every few months, and they copy each other quickly. What decides your results is experience: knowing what a model will do with a request, how much thinking to ask of it, and how to check what comes back.

Experience also compounds. Each new capability is worth more to someone who mastered the last one, and they get it before it becomes easy enough for everyone.

Since 2023 nearly all of Elliot’s work has gone into learning what the most capable AI systems can do. Coaching passes that experience on, so you spend less time working it out alone.

The shape of the work

One to one with individuals and small teams.

You meet with Elliot regularly, so he gets to know how you work and what you are trying to do, and each conversation builds on the last.

Bring the questions, decisions, and ideas in front of you. Or bring yourself, and Elliot will show you what is new since you last talked, or something older you have not tried yet.

Along the way you learn which model and settings suit each job, how to check the work, which news matters to you, and which features have been sitting in your tools unused.

Team training

Every new model is a head start and a new set of traps.

Elliot trains teams to work with AI agents that plan, act, and check their own results, from engineering teams building software to teams running the business.

When a new model drops, it can do things the last one could not, and the teams that learn them first have them before their competitors do. At the same time, some habits that worked with the last model start to cause problems.

One example: on the older Claude models, Elliot always asked for the highest thinking effort. On the current ones, that costs more and wanders from what you asked, and low or medium effort gives better answers. A team still on its old settings pays more for worse work.

Keeping the same workflow for a long time is the risky choice. We train teams to pick up each new capability early and to review their habits every time the models change.
