Before and after, the homepage at 1440

What Pictures keeps and drops

Keeps: every headline, every figure at page width, the four questions, the links, the flame room, the outcome band. Drops: the explanatory paragraphs and the section labels.

Three things exist only in this view, and every word in them is quoted from the copy they replace: an offer figure (BUILD / SUPPORT / OWN with "made to fit you", "a monthly subscription", "you own it outright"), a How it starts figure (four numbered circles, then a first version), and one caption on About. Privacy has no switch. No new claims, no external requests, the choice never leaves the browser.

How the judges moved it

Each round, three reviewers scored headless screenshots of all three pages at desktop and phone: one for the concept, one for visual craft, one for accessibility and code. The concept judge scored five questions out of 10.

All three said ship after round 2; round 3 applied their leftover fixes and stopped.

The second pass: "it feels unfinished"

Elliot's note after the first ship, plus the 8px phone captions, sent it through three more rounds with three fresh judges: one asked what makes it feel unfinished, one judged craft, one verified code and motion in a headless browser.

The finish judge's answer was precise: the figures had tripled but the headlines had not moved, the section labels were gone, and the crossfade read as a reload. So headlines now grow to poster size, the labels return above them as ember kickers, and the flip is a named view transition in which the masthead, each headline, and each figure travel to their new places while the paragraphs dissolve. The switch got a filled track and a glyph per option. The four questions became the poster of their section. The founder section joined the photo-and-caption recipe.

On phones the four wide homepage figures now re-stack their columns vertically at up to 1.4x with the arrows turned down; the sub pages' charts raise their type and carry two-line captions. The code judge caught two real problems in my first version of that (a breakpoint far too wide, and a spill over the next section in browsers without aspect-ratio), both fixed before merge, then verified motion end states, replay, the transition with animations on, JavaScript off, reduced motion, and no overflow from 320px up.

Known limits

  • The Pictures view is not indexed as a separate page and has no share image of its own. It is a reading preference, not a URL, though ?view=pictures links into it.
  • The finish judge's bolder version, one viewport per section with the words folding open on tap, would collapse the two views into one page with a disclosure state. Not built; it is a different product.

Where it lives

  • assets/view.js: wires the switch, remembers the choice, asks visible figures to redraw.
  • An inline script in the head of each page sets html[data-view="pictures"] before first paint.
  • The rules are the last block of assets/site.css. The README's file table documents the whole thing.
