Big pictures, few words: the site now has a Pictures view.

A switch in the masthead WordsPictures flips the public pages into posters: headline, picture, done. Same copy, nothing added, remembered on the device like light and dark.

  • Live: chamainteligente.com/?view=pictures
  • Issue #59
  • 3 judging rounds, 3 judges
  • 400 tests pass

Before and after, the homepage at 1440

What Pictures keeps and drops

Keeps: every headline, every figure at page width, the four questions, the links, the flame room, the outcome band. Drops: the explanatory paragraphs and the section labels.

Three things exist only in this view, and every word in them is quoted from the copy they replace: an offer figure (BUILD / SUPPORT / OWN with "made to fit you", "a monthly subscription", "you own it outright"), a How it starts figure (four numbered circles, then a first version), and one caption on About. Privacy has no switch. No new claims, no external requests, the choice never leaves the browser.

How the judges moved it

Each round, three reviewers scored headless screenshots of all three pages at desktop and phone: one for the concept, one for visual craft, one for accessibility and code. The concept judge scored five questions out of 10.

Do the pictures carry the story?57Offer became one wide figure; sections became single-column posters; figures grew, then to full width in round 3.
Are the words few enough?68Section labels and the masthead strap hidden; one-line meanings restored the subscription and ownership claims.

Is the switch legible?77Compact on phones, sharing the first masthead row; hero pentagon grown in round 3 so the flip is visible above the fold.

The craft judge found two Words-view regressions in round 1 (an extra masthead row on phones, a misaligned Members pill) and none in round 2. The accessibility judge verified keyboard operation, persistence, reduced motion, JavaScript off (the switch is not shown), that ?view= takes only the two literal values, and that hidden section headings still name their regions for a screen reader. Its one remaining functional finding, a :has() dependence in the training masthead, was replaced with a class in round 3. All three said ship after round 2; round 3 applied their leftover fixes and stopped.

Known limits

  • Sub-captions inside the figures render at 8 to 9px on a phone. The SVGs need larger text units or a stacked phone variant; CSS cannot fix it.
  • The Pictures view is not indexed as a separate page and has no share image of its own. It is a reading preference, not a URL, though ?view=pictures links into it.

Where it lives

  • assets/view.js: wires the switch, remembers the choice, asks visible figures to redraw.
  • An inline script in the head of each page sets html[data-view="pictures"] before first paint.
  • The rules are the last block of assets/site.css. The README's file table documents the whole thing.
