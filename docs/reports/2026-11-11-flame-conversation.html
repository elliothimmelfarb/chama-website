How it was done

  1. Read the real onesAll 38 saved conversations, measured for length and where they stopped.
  2. Built the measure13 conversation cases and 10 simulated visitors, run on the old prompt first.
  3. Rewrote the promptPurpose first, then how to talk, find the problem, and get them to talk to us. Facts kept word for word.
  4. Four roundsRead each failure, tightened one rule, ran it again.
  5. Reviewed and shipped

What changed in how it talks

  • Reply length follows the visitor's last message: a greeting gets a line, a long message a full answer
  • The first sentence answers the question asked
  • One question per reply, easy to answer in a few words
  • Answers in the visitor's language
  • The offer is always "talk to us", made the moment it is earned
  • Someone who asks for a person is asked for their details, not interviewed
  • Anything else (partnerships, press, jobs) is welcomed and passed on, with no invented policy
  • The note you receive carries what they want plus what the flame learned, in under 45 words

Open

  • "Talk to us" on the pageThe flame's offer now says it. The page's own words (the "What do you wish your software could do?" headline, the "Tell us what gets in your way" box, "start a conversation" on Coaching) are unchanged, since new public copy waits for you.
  • Two cases still flakeAbout 1 reply in 100 still asks a two-part question, usually the first reply to a one-line wish. The visitor who only asks "What is this?" stays to the end but still does not leave details.
  • Real visitorsEverything here is measured on simulated ones. The saved conversations from the next few weeks are the real test.
