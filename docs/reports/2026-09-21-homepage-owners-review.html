What I heard you ask for

Say the one idea first: software shaped for your business, instead of the business bending to software made for everyone. State the contract in one line, Annie's line. Describe the subscription as support and never as "whenever you ask". When new technology makes an improvement possible, say so and bring it in together. Say in the present that AI makes this affordable and fast, with no stories about the old days. Explain the process so people know what they sign up for. Keep the flame's question exactly as it is. And the register: less punchy, whole sentences, diplomatic, "business" not "company", the header's second tagline gone.

What changed visually

The display type came down by about a third and the letterspacing relaxed, so the page stops shouting. Ember is now an accent: one phrase in the offer headline, the outlines in the figures, the arrow after "Start here". Black rules became soft grey ones. The dark closing band is half its old height.

The shape moved to the top. In the hero, the business's irregular shape is drawn, a dashed square (software off the shelf) is tried on it and fades, and an ember outline is drawn that fits it exactly. The three parts of the offer carry small shape glyphs: drawn, fitted, filled. On a phone the headline comes first, the figure sits under the deck, and the offer stacks with an ember spine. Figure captions were enlarged so they read on a phone.
