Seven ways to open the home page on a phone

Today the first screen on a phone is five blocks of text, and the drawing that makes the site special starts at the very bottom edge. Each concept below makes the first screen mostly picture. They are mockups, nothing is built. Tell me which ones you like and I'll build from your picks.

Updated 2026-11-17: every mockup now leads with "We're ready to solve any problem that can be solved with software", your words from PR 208, which today sit as a heading halfway down home. "What you have to do, made easier" moves off the first screen (D keeps it as its two cards). The link preview image still carries the old line; I'd redraw it in the same change.

What a phone shows today

  • 5blocks of text before any picture: the descriptor line, the headline (two sentences), the paragraph, the question field and the scene sentence.
  • ~15%of the screen is drawing, a thin strip at the bottom edge, measured on a 390 by 844 phone.
  • 1place to act, the field in the middle of the screen, out of easy thumb reach.

Ideas that work with any of them

  • Field docked at the bottomThe Talk to us field sits in the thumb zone like a chat composer, and stays until you reach the doors. Every mockup above shows it.
  • Fewer words on the first screenThe descriptor line and the paragraph move to the second screen on phones only. Desktop keeps them where they are.
  • Ground tinted per conceptThe first screen could open on the tides or grid ground instead of paper, so the opening colour itself says something before you read.
