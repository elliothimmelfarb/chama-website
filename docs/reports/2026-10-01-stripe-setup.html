What I changed

  • Enabled SEPA Direct Debit and ACH Direct Debit.The cheapest way to collect monthly EU and US subscriptions. SEPA is pending Stripe's approval, with a €20,000 weekly limit to start.
  • Enabled Bank transfers.Each customer gets their own EUR, GBP or USD account number to pay into, with automatic matching and no chargebacks. Suited to the $2,500 setup fee, and a partial answer to the open USD-account question.
  • Enabled iDEAL | Wero (one-off and recurring) and Revolut Pay.
  • Set branding: the mark as the icon, ground #0e0c0a, ember #f4581f. This applies to Checkout, invoices, receipts and the customer portal.
  • Left the regional wallets (Korea, Brazil) on.They only appear when a price is shown in that country's currency, so they cost nothing.

Yours to click (the permission guard stopped me here)

  1. Verify the phone number by SMS. Settings → Business → Account details → Verify nowRequired to take payments from the Dashboard.
  2. Turn on automatic payouts, weekly on Monday. Settings → Linked accounts and payouts → Payout schedulePayouts are set to manual, so money would sit in Stripe until someone pays it out. Weekly gives the accountant one transfer a week to match.
  3. Turn on three customer emails: Successful payments, Refunds, and Bank transfers' payment instructions. Settings → Customer emailsWithout the last one, a customer who picks bank transfer never receives the account details.
  4. Enable Google Pay. This means accepting Google's API terms, which I won't do without your yes. Settings → Payment methods
  5. Stripe Tax: add the origin address and the Portugal registration. Tax → LocationsThe company is on IVA regime normal, so this records a fact rather than starting a new filing. It lets Checkout add 23% for Portuguese consumers and apply reverse charge for EU businesses.
  6. Authorize the Stripe MCP for the live account from an interactive claude terminal with /mcp.Now that the account is verified, Live appears in the environment picker.
  7. Later, once the code is deployed: create a restricted key (Checkout Sessions, Customers, Products and Prices, Invoices: write; Webhook endpoints: read) and one webhook endpoint per site. Then put each secret straight into Vercel with vercel env add. I'll give you the exact URLs and event lists.

Decisions

Managed Payments (you switched it on)

With Managed Payments, Stripe, through Link, becomes the seller of record: it charges VAT and sales tax worldwide and sends the customer's invoice. It covers ShopIntake's subscriptions but none of the human-delivered work. Stripe's rules exclude consulting and name "live 1-1 coaching" as ineligible, so the coaching, the Hearth packs and the done-for-you setup all stay on standard Checkout.

Leave it on, don't use it yet.

It is a per-checkout switch. Turn it on for ShopIntake when it starts selling self-serve to consumers abroad. Today ShopIntake's buyers are US businesses, so we owe no tax on those sales and the 3.5% would mostly buy the Link name on their statements.

One Stripe account or two

One account for now.

The descriptor and revenue lines can be separated by product and metadata. Add a second account in the Stripe organization once ShopIntake has its own brand and you want its own checkout look; it reuses this verification.

Certified faturas

Have the webhook issue each fatura through Moloni or InvoiceXpress.

Every card payment already emails you the details the accountant needs. The next step is to send each payment straight to a certified invoicing API, which needs the accountant's agreement on the software. The Terms and refund-policy pages Stripe expects are also still to write: I'll draft them in the brain for your approval before anything is published.
